PRECISION AND ANNOTATION AUDIT — 25 October 2026

Scope
Saved predictions only: original HRNet, mixed-data continuation, simple-first continuation. All 193 development studies, 248 source-listed CVC paths; source reconciliation also covers all 896 unique experiment training studies. No training, no checkpoint promotion, no label modification. Eight selected native-resolution crop panels personally inspected: six positive studies (largest paired Dice changes and all four previously recovered studies) and two highest-FP negative studies. Selection is diagnostic, not representative clinical sampling.

1. What actually went wrong
Positive-study macro precision: original 33.34%, mixed 25.14%, simple-first 23.00%. Pooled positive pixel precision: 30.72%, 23.87%, 21.74%. Positive pixel recall rises from 83.49% to 89.34% and 90.50%. More path detection comes with much more foreground outside the reference ribbon.
Positive false pixels: original 8,791,070; mixed 13,353,448; simple-first 15,287,168. These are measured against rasterized 10-native-pixel polylines, not clinically traced dense boundaries.
For simple-first, 24.17% of those false pixels are <=5 pixels outside the reference ribbon, 31.75% are >5 to15 pixels away, and 44.08% are >15 pixels away. Original proportions:34.41%,27.49%,38.10%. Mixed:27.01%,32.77%,40.22%. Thus neither pure boundary thickness error nor purely remote hallucination explains everything.
Only 0.79% of simple-first positive FP pixels overlap another device's annotated 10px ribbon. This does NOT prove other-device confusion is rare: those annotations are incomplete and narrow, and external wires/bone boundaries are not exhaustively labeled.
Negative false pixels: original25,692, mixed18,616, simple-first46,819. One study contributes42,469 (90.7%) of simple-first's total. With only eight negative studies, this is unstable as a population estimate.

Both continuations use positive-weighted BCE, Dice and an additional recall term; objective imbalance or feature drift are hypotheses requiring an ablation, not proven causes.

3. Annotation completeness: what is and is not verified
Exact coordinate-multiset reconciliation against original train_annotations.csv found zero lost/altered CVC polylines in 896 training studies (572 CVC instances) or193 validation studies (248 instances). No label-positive/no-polyline study or label-negative/with-polyline contradiction occurs in these cohorts.
This verifies conversion integrity, NOT that every visible catheter or full path was annotated. Inventory assessability/border flags are inherited metadata, not independent visual certification. 'Simple' means metadata-defined low clutter, not verified complete labels.
Across the entire study inventory,20,480 image-level CVC-positive studies have no CVC polyline. Never convert these into empty segmentation negatives. This count is outside the selected annotated experiment cohort; it does not explain a conversion failure here.
The prior training inspection's examples5 and9 remain annotation-completeness review candidates (short peripheral/neck traces). Example5 was re-inspected here at contact-sheet resolution: multiple visible line-like structures exist outside its single peripheral trace; their identity cannot safely be inferred from that alone. Additional visible lines are not automatically missing CVC labels.
RANZCR polylines provide a path reference; rasterizing each at fixed10px does not supply actual catheter wall boundaries. Near-ribbon FP may combine overwidth, annotation deviation, interpolation and genuine boundary uncertainty. Do not relabel all such pixels as correct or all distant pixels as wrong without review.

4. Threshold-only repair is unsafe as a blanket decision
Saved simple-first predictions evaluated at0.5/0.6/0.7 on four recovered paths. At0.5 ->0.7, path coverage changes56.15->47.10%,43.22->36.27%,92.16->81.01%,80.02->70.26%. All remain above the loose30% detection cutoff, concealing losses of7.0–11.2 percentage points. This is a targeted diagnostic, not a full-cohort threshold selection or deployment recommendation.
Do not remove small components blindly either: faint catheters are already fragmented. Do not keep only the largest component: multi-CVC paths are required.

5. Fix next, in order (nothing below has been trained)
A. Correct metric labels. Keep historical reports immutable. Report positive-only macro and pooled precision, native Dice, negative area per study, per-instance coverage and longest uncovered gaps separately. A 'missed count' alone is insufficient. Existing canonical precision/recall averages include negatives with special zero/one conventions; they must not be labeled positive-only accuracy.
B. Make a small adjudication queue from training-only data: high-confidence off-reference tracks, crossing branches, short/incomplete traces, and label-supported hard negatives. Include a blinded random sample as well. Ask a qualified reviewer only to mark confirmed CVC path, confirmed non-CVC foreground, or uncertain regions. Corrected labels must be versioned; uncertain regions must not become hard negatives. Development examples here are diagnostic and must not be copied into training.
C. Separate boundary uncertainty from confirmed background. Retain known positive centerline supervision and valid negative-study supervision. Apply any strong extra FP penalty only to confirmed non-CVC regions or reviewed-complete studies, with a training-defined boundary uncertainty band. The15px analysis bins are descriptive and are NOT a validated supervision margin.
D. Next single controlled experiment, once explicitly authorized: same mixed-data ledger, original architecture and budgets; compare unchanged recovery objective against a capped, image-normalized penalty for confirmed background false predictions. Keep the centerline/instance-recall protection identical. Do not simultaneously change resolution, backbone, sampling, threshold or several losses. Freeze separate original tip inference. Existing hard-negative image mining is not equivalent to this reviewed region-level supervision.
E. Gate progression against BOTH references: original model's precision/Dice/negative area and recovery model's per-instance coverage/gaps. Specifically track all four recovered paths and multi-CVC companions, not just total misses. Use a predeclared tolerance before training; do not choose it after seeing results. If both objectives cannot be met, retain separate candidates rather than declaring a new best model.
F. Expand negative evaluation before specificity claims. Eight negatives and one dominant failure are insufficient. Any newly assembled development set from previously exposed data remains development; independent clinical test claims require genuinely independent data and attested labels.

Recommendation
Keep the original model as the mask-quality reference and the recovery candidates as frozen diagnostic references. Prioritize reviewed region-level background supervision and boundary uncertainty, not more simple-only training, blanket threshold increases, automatic removal of difficult cases, or a new backbone. Model-wide 'perfect' segmentation and complete annotation accuracy remain unproven.
